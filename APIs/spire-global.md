### Spire Global (Spire Weather)

Weather API from Spire Global — a NYSE-listed space company that builds, owns, and operates its own constellation of 100+ LEMUR nanosatellites and runs its own global forecast model on the data they collect. Unusually for this catalog, Spire owns the entire chain from satellite to forecast: GNSS radio occultation profiles, ocean winds, and soil moisture feed a proprietary assimilation and NWP pipeline, surfaced through a REST API of point forecasts, GRIB2 file products, route forecasts, tides, lightning, and storm tracks. Enterprise-only — every route goes through sales.

- **Based in:** Vienna, Virginia, USA (Spire Global, Inc., NYSE: SPIR; founded 2012, public via SPAC merger 2021); satellite manufacturing in Glasgow, Scotland, with further offices including San Francisco, Boulder, Washington DC, Luxembourg, and Singapore
- **Access model:** Sales contact — no self-serve signup and no public price list. API keys are issued by the sales team and arrive in a welcome email; authentication is then a `spire-api-key` header. A live **API trial** is offered on request (typically one week), configured around your use case, chosen bundles, products, and forecast ranges. Notably, the full API documentation is public and readable without an account
- **Pricing model:** Custom / contact for pricing (subscription tiers exist and govern rate limits, but no figures are published at any level)
- **Coverage / data types:** ten endpoint families under `api.wx.spire.com` — **Point Forecasts** (`/forecast/point`, JSON for any lat/lon), **File Products** (GRIB2 regional/global grids), **Route Forecasts** (shipping routes and flight paths), **Current Weather** (`/current/weather/`, hourly surface observations at 3 km — dewpoint, cloud cover, shortwave radiation, MSLP, precipitation rate and type, humidity, temperature, visibility, u/v wind components, wind speed/direction/gust), **Archive** (historical data; the maritime historical product advertises 30 years including ocean currents and salinity), **Soil Moisture**, **Storm Tracks** (all active and historical tropical cyclones from 1990), **Tides**, **Lightning**, **WMS** (OGC Web Map Service layers), **Maritime Insights** (route-optimized marine forecasts), and **Custom Products**. Variables are sold in *bundles* — `basic`, `maritime`, `agricultural`, plus wind-energy (80/100/120 m AGL wind speeds) and solar-energy (GHI, DNI, plane-of-array irradiance) bundles on the Optimized Point Forecast. The Optimized Point Forecast is a distinct product for airports, ports, and selected weather stations, blending local sensors and ML, and updating hourly. No severe-weather-warning or radar-imagery product
- **Free tier:** none — access requires a sales conversation. The only no-cost route is the request-gated live trial, typically one week
- **Paid tiers:** custom / contact for pricing. Trial-configuration options give a sense of the product ladder: 15-day forecasts (6-hourly updates), 10-day (6-hourly), 7-day (6-hourly), 5-day (3-hourly), and 48-hour (hourly)
- **Rate limits:** not published — rate limiting is applied per subscription tier and returns HTTP 429; the specific thresholds are contract-dependent
- **Output formats:** JSON for point, current, and most data endpoints; GRIB2 for file products; OGC WMS layers for maps. A `unit_system` parameter switches between SI and US units, and an `X-Fields` header does server-side field filtering
- **Geographic strength:** genuinely global for the core model — 1/8° (~12 km) horizontal resolution worldwide, with radio occultation giving unusually good coverage over oceans and polar regions where ground observations are sparse. The 3 km **High-Resolution Forecast** is regional only: continental US, Europe, and southeast Asia, with hourly outputs to six days; custom domains and resolutions down to 1 km are available by arrangement
- **Notable limitations:** no self-serve access, no published pricing, and no ongoing free tier — this is an enterprise product aimed at energy and commodity traders, utilities, maritime operators, aviation, and agriculture, and it is impractical for hobbyists or quick prototyping; **the widely-quoted "3 km" figure applies only to the regional High-Resolution Forecast and Current Weather products, not the global model, which is ~12 km** — marketing pages and developer docs state these differently, so check which product a quoted resolution refers to; forecast data only completes updating roughly seven hours after issuance time and is served on a rolling basis, so a freshly-issued run may be partially populated (Spire documents this and provides a guide for handling it); only the past 3 days of forecasts are retained in the API by default, with deeper history by request; variables are gated into bundles, so the parameter set depends on what you bought; no severe-weather alerts or radar display imagery; dissemination timing is explicitly not guaranteed and can slip when upstream partners are delayed
- **Built on:** Spire's own vertically-integrated pipeline — the company states it owns and operates every stage from satellites and ground stations through data assimilation to the global forecast model. Proprietary space-based inputs are GNSS radio occultation (atmospheric temperature, pressure, and humidity profiles), GNSS-reflectometry ocean winds, and soil moisture, collected by its own LEMUR nanosatellite constellation and now supplemented by a Hyperspectral Microwave Sounder demonstrator. These are merged with third-party sources including NOAA, EUMETSAT, and ECMWF, with convective-scale data assimilation and "hot-start" cloud and radar ingestion on the high-resolution regional domains, plus ML enrichment on the Optimized Point Forecast. Spire is also an upstream supplier in its own right, selling radio occultation data to NOAA and EUMETSAT for use in *their* operational forecasts
