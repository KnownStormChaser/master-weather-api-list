### DTN

Enterprise weather data APIs and bulk feeds from DTN — a Minnesota-based data company that assembled much of the private weather industry by acquisition (MeteoGroup, Weather Decision Technologies, Frontier Weather, Meteorlogix) and now bills itself as the world's largest private weather organization. Seven REST APIs cover forecasts, 70,000+ station observations, map tiles, lightning, renewables, tropical cyclones, and agency bulletins, alongside a large catalog of gridded NetCDF and shapefile data feeds. No self-serve access and no published pricing at any tier.

- **Based in:** Bloomington, Minnesota, USA (DTN, LLC; the Minneapolis–Burnsville area, founded 1984 in Omaha, Nebraska). Owned by **TBG AG**, a private investment holding company headquartered in Zurich, Switzerland, which bought DTN from Schneider Electric in 2017 for $900M. 24/7 weather operations centres in Minneapolis, Norman (Oklahoma), Utrecht, Sydney, and Manila
- **Access model:** Sales contact — no self-serve signup, no public price list, and no way to obtain a key without a business conversation (every call to action is a "Contact Us" lead form). A developer portal exists at `devportal.dtn.com` with an API catalog, interactive documentation, and Swagger/OpenAPI specifications, but the catalog renders via JavaScript and access is account-gated. DTN also lists several of its weather APIs on AWS Marketplace, but those listings are marked **"Private Offer Only"** — so even the marketplace route runs through DTN sales
- **Pricing model:** Custom / contact for pricing (no published figures at any level, on DTN's own site or via AWS Marketplace)
- **Coverage / data types:** *Seven REST APIs* — **Weather Conditions** (worldwide forecasts, current conditions, historical), **Observation** (near-real-time and historical station data from 70,000+ public and private stations, atmospheric/road/agricultural/marine parameters, quality-controlled with optional model gap-filling; up to 30 days per request from as far back as 30 years), **Map Tile** (vector and raster layers), **Lightning** (worldwide near-real-time and historical), **Renewables** (location-specific environmental data for wind/solar), **Tropical Cyclone** (past, current, and forecast positions globally), and **Agency Bulletin** (global weather bulletin text). *Bulk data feeds, sold separately* — gridded global forecast model output (NetCDF, to 0.1° resolution, 15-day horizon), historical gridded weather (2013–present), historical lightning (September 2019–present), radar (masked base reflectivity and future radar), global precipitation-rate radar (5-minute updates), global radar mosaic, satellite (Europe/North America/global), night fog (10-minute updates, 4 km), MetStorm QPE (global hourly radar-based precipitation estimates), weather fronts (GeoJSON, 3-hourly), plus CONUS-only products — EchoTops, hail swaths, rotation tracks, storm attributes, and storm corridors
- **Free tier:** none published — there is no free tier, trial figure, or evaluation quota documented on any publicly accessible page
- **Paid tiers:** custom / contact for pricing
- **Rate limits:** not published — DTN states only that the APIs handle high-volume requests "with rate limiting safeguards"; actual thresholds are contract-dependent
- **Output formats:** GeoJSON for the REST APIs (DTN highlights GeoJSON-parsable responses across its API line); NetCDF for gridded, radar, and satellite feeds; shapefile for hail swaths and rotation tracks; CSV for storm attributes. Client SDKs are offered for Python, JavaScript, and Java, and webhooks can push data during significant weather events
- **Geographic strength:** global — worldwide forecasts, observations, lightning, and tropical cyclones, with 24/7 forecast operations on three continents and the MeteoGroup acquisition adding deep European coverage. Several radar-derived products are **CONUS-only** (EchoTops, hail swaths, rotation tracks, storm attributes), and satellite feeds are split by region
- **Notable limitations:** no self-serve access, no published pricing, and no documented free tier — this is an enterprise sales product, impractical for hobbyists or quick evaluation, and the AWS Marketplace listings are private-offer-only rather than a self-serve shortcut; the public developer portal catalog and the data-feeds catalog are both JavaScript-rendered and effectively gated, so endpoint-level detail, parameter lists, and rate limits can't be reviewed before contacting sales; the product estate is unusually sprawling and brand-fragmented after years of acquisitions (WeatherSentry, WeatherOps, ClearAg, ClearPath, Frontier Weather, MetConsole and more sit alongside the raw APIs), which makes it genuinely hard to work out what a given contract covers; the APIs and the bulk data feeds are separate purchases; several radar products are CONUS-only; DTN does not publicly enumerate the NWP models behind its forecasts
- **Built on:** DTN's own forecast operation rather than a resold feed — the company runs in-house numerical modelling and post-processing staffed by 200+ meteorologists across its global centres, built up from the MeteoGroup (2019), Weather Decision Technologies (2018), Frontier Weather, and Meteorlogix acquisitions, and blends model output with its 70,000+ station observation network, radar, satellite, and its own lightning detection data. Specific underlying NWP models are not enumerated publicly
