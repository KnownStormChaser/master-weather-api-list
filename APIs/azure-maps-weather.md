### Azure Maps Weather

Microsoft's weather service, delivered as one metered meter within Azure Maps — an unusually broad endpoint set covering current conditions, forecasts from minute-by-minute out to 45 days, severe alerts, tropical storms, air quality, climatology, activity indices, and weather-along-route. The underlying data is AccuWeather's, surfaced through Azure's REST interface and billing.

- **Based in:** Redmond, Washington, USA (Microsoft Corporation)
- **Access model:** Instant self-serve — create an Azure subscription and an Azure Maps account, then authenticate with a subscription key or Microsoft Entra ID; no approval step. Volume discounts and Enterprise Agreement pricing go through sales contact
- **Pricing model:** Freemium (ongoing monthly free transaction allotment per meter, then pay-as-you-go per 1,000 transactions with volume discounts; EA/custom by contract)
- **Coverage / data types:** an unusually wide endpoint set — current conditions (plus past 6/24-hour observations), daily forecast (1, 5, 10, 15, 25, or 45 days), hourly forecast (1–240 hours), quarter-day forecast (1–15 days), minute forecast (next 120 minutes at 1/5/15-minute intervals, precipitation type and dBZ intensity), severe weather alerts (worldwide, from government meteorological agencies plus regional providers), daily indices (activity suitability — sport, construction, farming), air quality (current, hourly to 96 hours, daily to 7 days, with pollutant levels and precautions), daily historical actuals, normals, and records, tropical storms (active, forecast, locations, search), and weather along route with hazard assessment. Weather map tiles are billed as a separate meter. Response fields include RealFeel™ and RealFeel Shade™ temperature, wet-bulb, dew point, ceiling, visibility, pressure tendency, cloud cover, UV index, degree-day summaries, and pollen/mold counts
- **Free tier:** ongoing — 1,000 free Weather transactions/month under Gen2 pricing, resetting each billing cycle (Weather Tiles carry a separate 1,000/month allotment); new Azure accounts also get a $200 credit for 30 days. No standalone free key exists outside an Azure subscription
- **Paid tiers:** pay-as-you-go per 1,000 transactions beyond the free allotment, with volume discounts starting at 100,000 transactions/month. **Exact per-1,000 rates are not published as static text** — the pricing table renders its figures dynamically and varies by Azure region and billing currency, so the Azure pricing calculator or EA portal is the authoritative source. Enterprise Agreement and negotiated pricing via sales contact
- **Rate limits:** per-service QPS limits apply and return HTTP 429 when exceeded; Gen2 removes the tighter Gen1 S0 query-per-second restrictions and QPS ceilings can be raised via an Azure portal support request. No maximum daily transaction cap. For weather map tiles, one billable transaction is counted per 15 tile requests
- **Output formats:** JSON (REST); weather map tiles as raster/vector tiles
- **Geographic strength:** global — severe weather alerts are sourced worldwide from national meteorological agencies plus regional alert providers, and forecast coverage inherits AccuWeather's global footprint
- **Notable limitations:** **Gen1 pricing (S0/S1) is deprecated and retires 15 September 2026**, after which remaining Gen1 accounts are migrated to Gen2 automatically — widely reported as a roughly 9× per-transaction increase for typical S0 customers, so any cost modelling based on older figures is stale; forecast horizons were tier-gated under Gen1 (S0 capped daily forecasts at 15 days and hourly at 72 hours, with 25/45-day and 120/240-hour reserved for S1/Gen2). Requires an Azure subscription and Azure Maps account, so setup overhead is higher than a plain API-key service; per-1,000 prices aren't available as published static text and shift by region and currency; billing is transaction-based with service-specific counting rules (tiles at 15 requests per transaction) that complicate estimation; the weather data is licensed from AccuWeather rather than modelled by Microsoft, so accuracy and coverage track that upstream provider; no marine/tide, radar-imagery-data, or deep sub-daily historical archive endpoints
- **Built on:** AccuWeather — disclosed directly in API responses via a `sources: ["AccuWeather"]` field and by the presence of AccuWeather's RealFeel™ temperature indices; severe weather alerts additionally aggregate official government meteorological agency warnings and leading global-to-regional alert providers
- **Docs:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/maps/weather
