### Google Weather API

Weather endpoint of Google Maps Platform, serving current conditions, 10-day daily and 240-hour hourly forecasts, 24-hour history, and alerts from Google's own forecast stack — blending traditional NWP with DeepMind/Google Research neural weather models. Sold as one metered SKU inside the wider Maps Platform rather than as a standalone weather product.

- **Based in:** Mountain View, California, USA (Google LLC, a subsidiary of Alphabet Inc.)
- **Access model:** Instant self-serve — create a Google Cloud project, enable the Weather API, attach a billing account, and generate a key; a **Maps Demo Key** lets you try the API with no credit card and no billing account. Enterprise volumes and custom terms go through sales contact
- **Pricing model:** Freemium (ongoing 10,000 free calls/month on the Weather SKU, then pay-as-you-go; fixed-price subscription bundles and Enterprise custom also available)
- **Coverage / data types:** current conditions, daily forecast (up to 10 days), hourly forecast (up to 240 hours), hourly history (up to 24 hours of cached past conditions), and weather alerts; the docs additionally cover a minute forecast and weather map tiles. Elements include temperature (min/max/apparent), precipitation amount and type (rain/snow/ice/mix), wind (speed, direction, gust, chill), humidity/dew point/heat index/wet-bulb, UV index, thunderstorm probability, visibility, sea-level pressure, ice thickness, cloud cover, and sun/moon rise-set events. Air quality and pollen are **separate Maps Platform APIs**, not part of this one
- **Free tier:** ongoing — 10,000 free calls/month on the **Weather Usage** SKU (`9DB8-727A-ACFE`), renewing monthly; new Google Cloud customers also get a $300 trial credit, and the Maps Demo Key allows no-cost prototyping without billing details
- **Paid tiers:** pay-as-you-go per 1,000 calls after the free cap, tiered by monthly volume — $0.15 (to 100K), $0.12 (100K–500K), $0.09 (500K–1M), $0.06 (1M–5M), $0.038 (5M+). Alternatively, fixed-price Maps Platform subscriptions that bundle Weather with other products: Essentials $275/mo (100,000 monthly calls), Pro $1,200/mo (250,000); Enterprise custom / contact for pricing. Overage on a subscription is billed as pay-as-you-go
- **Rate limits:** no per-minute quota published on the public pricing or Weather documentation pages; usage is metered per month against the free cap and per-SKU volume tiers, and per-project quotas are configurable in the Google Cloud console
- **Output formats:** JSON (REST)
- **Geographic strength:** broad international coverage by lat/lon, but **not universal** — see limitations for the excluded countries. Forecast/current/history cover most of the world; alerts are limited to roughly 45 countries
- **Notable limitations:** coverage is country-gated and the gaps are significant — China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, and Syria return **no** current conditions, forecast, history, or alerts, while Japan, South Korea, and Vietnam are **alerts-only** (no current conditions, forecast, or history); Canada has forecast data but no alerts. History is only a 24-hour rolling window (no deep archive), and the forecast tops out at 10 days / 240 hours. No marine, tide, radar-imagery, or agriculture products, and air quality and pollen are billed as separate APIs. Requires a Google Cloud project and billing account for production use, so it carries more setup overhead than a plain API-key service; use is governed by the Google Maps Platform Terms of Service, and product availability, functionality, and terms differ for customers billed in the European Economic Area (EEA). Google positions this as a *low-resolution*, developer-friendly product — the raw high-resolution AI model output is a different offering (WeatherNext on Earth Engine/BigQuery)
- **Built on:** Google's own weather forecast — data from official sources combined with a proprietary AI-powered prediction algorithm, with forecast models enhanced by neural weather models from Google DeepMind and Google Research (the WeatherNext lineage) alongside traditional numerical weather prediction; current and forecast data refresh roughly every 15–30 minutes
