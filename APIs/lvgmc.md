### LVĢMC (Latvian Environment, Geology and Meteorology Centre)

Latvia's national meteorological and hydrological service, publishing forecasts, observations, lightning, and warnings as open datasets on the national open-data portal rather than through a purpose-built weather API. There is no developer portal and no API key — instead, CKAN's DataStore exposes every table to SQL queries over HTTP, so a forecast for one town or one coordinate is a single URL. Everything is released into the public domain under CC0.

- **Based in:** Riga, Latvia (Latvijas Vides, ģeoloģijas un meteoroloģijas centrs, a state-owned company acting as the national hydrometeorological service). The API layer itself is Latvia's open data portal, `data.gov.lv`, operated by the State Digital Development Agency
- **Access model:** No key required — public HTTPS endpoints on the national open-data portal, no signup. Data is reachable two ways: bulk CSV download per resource, or the CKAN DataStore API (`/api/3/action/datastore_search` and `datastore_search_sql`), which accepts arbitrary read-only SQL against each table and returns JSON. LVĢMC pushes each new forecast to the portal as soon as it is produced
- **Pricing model:** Free (open public-service data; no paid tiers)
- **Coverage / data types:** six datasets. **Populated-place forecasts** — hourly for the next 24 hours and daily for the next 7 days across roughly 6,500 Latvian populated places, filtered by `CITY_ID`, with a place list, a 20-entry parameter list, and a weather-pictogram lookup table. **Spatial forecasts** — 9-day gridded forecasts queryable by coordinate, bounding box, or nearest grid point, covering air temperature, apparent temperature, relative humidity, mean-sea-level pressure, hourly precipitation, precipitation phase, precipitation probability, wind speed, direction and maximum gust, total cloud cover, fog intensity index, thunderstorm probability, snow depth, probability of sub-zero 2 m temperature, and UV index. **Hydrological forecasts** — 14-day daily ensemble forecasts of water temperature and water level at LVĢMC hydrological observation points, published as the ensemble median plus three spread bands (full range, 25–75%, and 5–95%), with levels in metres above sea level in the Latvian LAS-2000,5 height system. **Hydrometeorological observations** — meteorological and hydrological station measurements for the past 48 hours, a 365-day archive (both actuals and daily avg/min/max), observed weather phenomena, plus station and parameter metadata. **Lightning grid** — hourly aggregated lightning counts on a 5×5 km grid for the rolling past 24 hours, split into cloud-to-cloud, cloud-to-ground, and total strikes per cell. **Hydrometeorological warnings** — warning metadata with phenomenon, onset and end time, impact assessment and advice, joined to affected municipalities and to warning polygons
- **Free tier:** everything is free with no key and no published quota. Forecast horizons are 24 hours hourly and 7 days daily for places, 9 days for the grid, and 14 days for the hydrological ensemble; the observation archive runs 365 days and the lightning grid 24 hours
- **Paid tiers:** none
- **Rate limits:** not published. Practical limits are those of the portal's CKAN DataStore rather than anything LVĢMC states
- **Output formats:** JSON from the DataStore API; CSV for whole-resource downloads
- **Geographic strength:** Latvia only — populated places, grid, stations, lightning cells, and warning areas are all within the country
- **Notable limitations:** this is a national open-data catalogue, not a weather API — there is no developer portal, no versioned endpoints, no status page, and the query layer, uptime, and any rate limiting belong to the portal operator rather than to the met service. **LVĢMC states that data sent to data.gov.lv receives only basic validation, so portal values can differ from the verified data held in LVĢMC's own systems** — a meaningful caveat for anything operational. Data comes back in long format: one row per location, parameter, and timestamp, with the value in a `VERTIBA` column, so assembling a usable forecast means joining against the lookup tables and pivoting client-side. Field names, parameter descriptions, and all dataset documentation are Latvian-only. Timebases differ between products: the populated-place forecasts are published in Latvian local time while the spatial forecast and lightning grid are in UTC. Update frequency is listed as irregular, with the spatial forecast guaranteed only four times a day and the precipitation-probability layer on its own schedule. Resolution varies within the spatial dataset — the precipitation-probability, sub-zero-probability, and UV layers use a coarser grid. Lightning is aggregated to 5 km cells and one-hour bins rather than individual strikes, retained only 24 hours, and each cell's coordinates give the **south-west corner**, not the centre. Hydrological forecast points can be withdrawn indefinitely if a station has technical problems, and water levels use the LAS-2000,5 height system, which needs converting for use with other datums. The observation archive is capped at 365 days, and there are no radar, satellite, air-quality, or marine products. Warnings are issued per administrative municipality or polygon rather than per coordinate, and Riga is warned separately from the region containing it, so a naive lookup can return two overlapping warnings for the capital. All datasets are CC0 1.0, so no attribution is legally required (crediting LVĢMC remains good practice)
- **Built on:** LVĢMC states that its forecasters combine output from several international numerical weather prediction centres and apply both algorithmic and manual post-processing before verifying and publishing; the specific source models are not named. The hydrological forecasts come from LVĢMC's own hydrological model, driven by hydrological and meteorological observations together with ensemble output from a numerical weather prediction model, with manual post-processing as needed. Observations come from LVĢMC's own meteorological, hydrological, and lightning-detection networks, and warnings are written by its duty synoptic meteorologists and hydrologists
